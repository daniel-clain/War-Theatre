## 🧠 Core Deductive Techniques for a Medieval Fantasy Detective

These are the reasoning techniques that can be used to piece together the truth from unreliable information, limited resources, and human behavior.

---

### 🔁 1. **Contradiction Detection**

> The foundation of deductive reasoning: find statements, timelines, or behaviors that cannot logically coexist.

- **Mutual Exclusivity**: Two claims or facts cannot both be true (e.g. “He was alone” vs. “She saw him with someone”).
- **Temporal Contradiction**: A person claims to be in two places at once, or at the wrong time for the event.
- **Behavioral Inconsistency**: Their emotional state or actions don’t match their claim (e.g., overly defensive, feigned surprise).
- **Physical Incompatibility**: The action they describe requires strength, tools, or access they didn’t have.

---

### 🧹 2. **Elimination Reasoning**

> Narrowing down suspects or possibilities by ruling out those who _could not_ have done it.

- **Alibi Validation**: Verifying someone's claimed location by independent or indirect means (e.g., someone saw them elsewhere).
- **Lack of Access**: They physically could not reach the location (locked door, guarded gate).
- **Lack of Capability**: They lacked strength, knowledge, or motive to perform the act.
- **Logical Exhaustion**: All other suspects accounted for—only one remains.

---

### 🔗 3. **Causal Necessity (If-Then Reasoning)**

> Drawing conclusions based on dependencies: if _this_ is true, _that_ must follow.

- **Exclusive Cause**: Only one action could’ve led to the outcome—who could’ve done it?
- **Implied Action**: If a body was moved, someone must have carried it—who could’ve?
- **Reversed Outcome**: If someone tampered with the door, there _must_ be evidence of it—if there isn't, then they didn’t.

---

### 🧭 4. **Opportunity Analysis**

> Was the suspect in the right place, at the right time, with the ability to act?

- **Time Window Compression**: The act could only have been committed within a narrow time frame—who had the chance?
- **Hidden Travel Path**: Someone without an apparent route may have used a forgotten passage or unseen entry.
- **Tool / Resource Availability**: The act required something specific—poison, fire, tools. Who had access?

---

### 👁️ 5. **Observational Discrepancy**

> Sensory evidence or material signs contradict a witness’s account.

- **Environmental Clues**: Dust, blood, footprints, weather, torchlight, etc., that reveal hidden activity.
- **Overlooked Details**: A character mentions something only the perpetrator would know.
- **Anomalous Absence**: Something expected is missing (e.g., no cup on a table someone said they drank from).

---

### 🎭 6. **Motivation Alignment**

> Evaluating whether someone had sufficient reason—emotional, financial, social—to commit the act.

- **Obvious Motive**: Greed, revenge, fear, jealousy.
- **Hidden Motive**: Shameful secrets, political leverage, family honor, prophecy.
- **Non-Criminal Deception**: A suspect lies for unrelated reasons (e.g., an affair, escaping taxes), but this clouds the truth.

---

### 🧩 7. **Relational Revelation**

> Discovering that people are connected in ways they hide—often the key to motive or collusion.

- **Secret Bonds**: Hidden family ties, lovers, blood oaths, shared debts.
- **Shared History**: Former guildmates, common military service, exile from same town.
- **Power Dynamics**: Who benefits if the victim dies? Who rises in rank, inherits land, or silences blackmail?

---

### 🔦 8. **False or Faulty Witnessing**

> Interrogating the reliability and limits of human perception and memory.

- **Mistaken Identity**: A cloaked figure was misrecognized. Darkness or distance distorted memory.
- **Memory Corruption**: Witnesses forget, confabulate, or are manipulated.
- **Bias Blindness**: A witness lies without realizing—because they assume things that fit their worldview.

---

### 🧪 9. **Unknowable Detail Slip**

> A classic tell: someone reveals knowledge they shouldn't have.

- **Too Much Detail**: They “guess” specifics only the culprit would know (e.g., “he was stabbed in the back” before anyone said how).
- **Corrected Lie**: They “fix” their testimony to match later facts—suggesting foreknowledge.
- **Premature Reaction**: They react to news they weren’t supposed to hear yet.

---

### 🧵 10. **Chain Reconstruction**

> Piecing together a timeline or sequence of events to find anomalies or hidden steps.

- **Broken Sequence**: The timeline skips a moment, or something happens too fast.
- **Overlap Error**: Two events supposedly simultaneous, but one had to follow the other.
- **Transitive Clues**: X gives Y to Z—who had it when? When did it move?

---

### ⏳ 11. **Recontextualization**

> Reinterpreting known facts under a new light—often the final “aha” leap.

- **Reversal of Role**: The victim was the aggressor. The servant was the mastermind.
- **Symbolic Meaning**: The object wasn’t random—it was a message, a ritual, a signature.
- **Pattern Reframing**: What seemed like chaos was actually a code or staged sequence.
