# ☠️ Valid Large-Scale Threat Types (Through the Veil)

This document defines the **principles, rules, and constraints** for designing valid large-scale threats in _Through the Veil_. These constraints are critical for ensuring that threats:

- Enable strong investigative gameplay.
- Support logical deduction chains.
- Motivate player-driven discovery, rather than reactive defense.

---

## 🧠 Core Definition

A valid large-scale threat in _Through the Veil_ is:

- An event that would result in the **death or transformation of many innocents**.
- **Not yet triggered** — it depends on **specific preconditions** or preparations.
- **Not obvious** at first — its true nature must be **discovered through investigation**.
- **Preventable** — the player can uncover and interrupt the plan _before_ it unfolds.

---

## 📏 Design Rules for Valid Threats

### 1. **Must Be Latent, Not Immediate**

- The threat must **not have already occurred**.
- It should be in the process of being **prepared, summoned, constructed, or spread**.
- The delay allows players to investigate, gather information, and intervene.

**Invalid:** A dragon is already attacking the town.
**Valid:** A ritual is being prepared to summon a dragon at midnight.

---

### 2. **Must Be Existential in Scope**

- The threat must affect a **large number of innocent people**.
- It should feel **irreversible** once triggered — death, corruption, transformation, or enslavement.
- Stakes must be high enough to justify full investigation and urgency.

**Invalid:** A minor theft ring targeting market stalls.
**Valid:** A plague seeded in the water supply that will kill hundreds.

---

### 3. **Must Require Preparation Conditions**

- The threat depends on a **series of specific conditions** being met:

  - Ritual completion
  - Artifact assembly
  - Timing (e.g., celestial events)
  - Environmental changes (e.g., water tainting, soil corruption)

- These dependencies allow the player to **deduce the plan step-by-step**.

**Invalid:** The town will randomly explode tomorrow.
**Valid:** A buried explosive is armed, but requires three ignition relics to activate.

---

### 4. **Must Be Hidden, but Detectable**

- The player cannot be explicitly told what the threat is.
- Instead, the world must offer **indirect evidence**:

  - Anomalous behavior
  - Missing people
  - Strange weather, animals, or symptoms
  - Rumors, patterns, small-scale signs

- These clues must be logically connected to the threat and uncoverable through investigation.

**Invalid:** A god descends from the sky with no warning.
**Valid:** A cult is secretly preparing to open a god-summoning gate, and their symbols are appearing across town.

---

### 5. **Must Support Multiple Reasoning Paths**

- Players should be able to:

  - Observe anomalies
  - Form competing theories
  - Rule out false leads
  - Triangulate the truth through logic

- There should be **no single mandatory path** to the solution — multiple deduction chains should converge on the same answer.

**Invalid:** The only way to solve the mystery is to talk to a single NPC.
**Valid:** The player can deduce the danger from maps, missing shipments, or strange rituals — each path reinforcing the others.

---

### 6. **Must Be Interruptible Through Specific Actions**

- The threat must have **concrete vulnerabilities** the player can exploit once discovered:

  - Kill the cultist before the ritual
  - Seal the rift with a counter-spell
  - Remove one of the three required items

- Resolution must be **logical**, not based on arbitrary triggers.

**Invalid:** Once discovered, the threat auto-cancels.
**Valid:** Discovery enables targeted intervention.

---

## 🎯 Design Summary Template

Use this structure to design threats:

> **Threat Summary**: A brief one-line overview.
>
> **Trigger Condition**: What causes the threat to take effect.
>
> **Preparation Requirements**: The steps/resources/conditions that must be fulfilled before the threat can trigger.
>
> **Concealment Mechanism**: Why the threat isn’t already obvious.
>
> **Evidence Trail**: What clues or anomalies reveal the existence of the threat.
>
> **Disruption Methods**: What specific actions can prevent the threat from triggering.

---

## ✅ Final Guidelines

- Threats should feel **plausible, serious, and preventable**.
- The more **indirect and multi-step** the preparation, the better the mystery potential.
- Every threat must be something that a **deductive, rational player can unravel logically**.
- A good threat should initially feel like "something is wrong" — then become terrifying once the full plan is uncovered.

This template should be used as a **filter and quality standard** when generating large-scale threats in _Through the Veil_.
