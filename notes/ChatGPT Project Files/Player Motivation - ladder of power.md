# Gameplay – Player Motivation & Ladder of Power

## Core Concept: The Ladder of Power

The **Ladder of Power** represents a player's changing influence, agency, and access within the game world. It is the central metaphor for **player motivation**, tracking their ascent or descent based on the consequences of their actions (or inaction).

- Moving **up** the ladder: Gain power, options, control, and influence.
- Moving **down** the ladder: Lose autonomy, access, effectiveness, and become increasingly reactive or desperate.

Player position on the ladder is not fixed — it changes dynamically in response to how well they handle:

- **Threats** (avoiding or neutralizing negative consequences)
- **Opportunities** (seizing chances for gain or leverage)

## The Exponential Spiral Model

Motivation is driven by an exponential feedback loop:

| Direction           | Reinforcement Pattern                                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Upward Spiral**   | The more the player gets what they want and avoids what they don’t want, the easier it becomes to continue succeeding. |
| **Downward Spiral** | The more the player suffers what they don’t want and loses what they value, the harder it becomes to recover.          |

This design rewards:

- **Smart decision-making**
- **Long-term planning**
- **Strategic prioritization of limited resources and time**

## Player Wants vs. Avoidances

Player behavior is motivated by two forces:

### 🎯 Things Players Want:

- **Power** – ability to influence major events
- **Resources** – tools, allies, knowledge, wealth
- **Information** – to make better decisions
- **Autonomy** – freedom of choice and strategy
- **Status** – being respected or feared
- **Truth** – uncovering the hidden structure of the world
- **Purpose** – fulfilling character goals or story arcs

### 🚫 Things Players Want to Avoid:

- **Being controlled** – losing freedom, manipulated
- **Weakness** – unable to act or defend
- **Ignorance** – acting on false or missing information
- **Lack of options** – trapped or cornered
- **Fear** – constant threat or instability
- **Compromise** – being forced to act against one’s values
- **Obscurity** – being irrelevant or unimpactful

The ladder is a **gradient of fulfillment**:

- Low rungs = fear, scarcity, survival
- Mid rungs = stability, planning, partial influence
- High rungs = power, strategy, world-shaping consequences

## Gameplay Loop Tied to Motivation

The player's core gameplay loop reinforces this motivation model:

1. **Observe** – Spot anomalies, threats, or emerging opportunities.
2. **Investigate** – Gather partial information or test assumptions.
3. **Deduce** – Apply logic to form high-quality insights.
4. **Decide** – Take a calculated action based on limited info.
5. **React** – Adapt to consequences or escalating threats.

If the player succeeds, they:

- Remove threats before they become problems
- Exploit openings others missed
- Improve their power, reputation, or strategic position

If the player fails:

- Threats progress into consequences
- Mistakes snowball into more problems
- Others outmaneuver them

## Idle Players Fall Behind

Inaction has a cost:

- **Threats grow unchecked**
- **Opportunities are seized by others**
- **World events progress without them**

The game is built to reward proactive behavior and punish complacency — not through artificial timers, but through the **logical consequences of a living world**.

## Strategic Intelligence Is the Key Skill

At its core, the game challenges players to:

- Recognize what matters
- Prioritize what’s winnable
- Solve real problems using logic
- Leverage what they know, and learn what they don't

The best player isn’t the one who clicks the fastest — it’s the one who thinks the clearest.

## Summary: Motivation Model Principles

- Every player action (or inaction) shifts their place on the ladder.
- Motivation is tied to clarity: players must discover what will improve or harm their position.
- Information is a **resource**, and the **ability to interpret and act on it** is the primary skill.
- All game systems (threats, opportunities, reputation, options) feed into this ladder framework.
