# 🧭 Player Crisis Response Framework

When

---

## 🧠 Core Philosophy

Players will approach threats:

- Rationally
- Logically
- With minimal assumptions
- Using real-world-inspired strategies

They will **not choose strange or roundabout approaches** when a direct, practical path is available. Players treat each threat as a real problem that needs solving efficiently, safely, and reliably.

---

## 🎯 Phase-Based Player Response Strategy

### Phase 1: Clarify and Confirm the Threat

- Player will **not act prematurely** unless the threat is obvious.
- If signs are vague, player will:

  - Collect facts and verify information.
  - Identify what is known vs unknown.
  - Compare observed evidence to plausible theories.

**Priority Context:** Early uncertainty, ambiguous evidence, unclear consequences.

---

### Phase 2: Assess Trust Landscape

- Player evaluates:

  - Can the **authorities be trusted**?
  - Can **specific individuals** be relied on?
  - Will **public knowledge** help or harm the solution?

**Priority Context:** When betrayal, incompetence, or cult infiltration are possible.

---

### Phase 3: Decide Between Secrecy or Alarm

- If trust is high and threat is clear: **Alert leaders and organize response**.
- If trust is low or threat is still unclear: **Investigate quietly**.
- Player will avoid tipping off enemies if secrecy is advantageous.

**Priority Context:** When miscommunication, public panic, or sabotage is a risk.

---

### Phase 4: Investigate the Source and Scope

- Player will seek:

  - Key witnesses and experts.
  - Places where the anomaly first occurred.
  - Patterns in events or symptoms.

- Focus is on **gaining understanding** before committing to action.

**Priority Context:** When immediate containment is impossible or misdirected.

---

### Phase 5: Secure What Can Be Secured

- If threat location is known:

  - Secure water supply, defensive structures, ritual sites, etc.
  - Block access, quarantine areas, guard critical zones.

- If threat is unknown:

  - Secure vulnerable information, assets, and personnel.

**Priority Context:** When a risk can be delayed, isolated, or slowed.

---

### Phase 6: Mobilize Resources and Help

- Player will identify:

  - Specialists (healers, priests, mages, scholars).
  - Allies or institutions that can help investigate or contain.

- Will only do this **once initial clarity is achieved**.

**Priority Context:** When threat nature is known, but action requires expertise.

---

### Phase 7: Contain and Neutralize

- Player takes direct action only when:

  - They know what the threat is.
  - They know where it is.
  - They believe the solution is within reach.

**Priority Context:** When risk is urgent, imminent, or preventable.

---

## 📌 Prompt Design Implications

Scenario prompts must:

- **Withstand logical scrutiny** — the villain's plan must require secrecy, preparation, and can’t be immediately solved by obvious action.
- **Account for rational priorities** — if the player can immediately solve the crisis by acting directly, it must be justified (e.g., it's a trap, misdirection, or just the start of a larger mystery).
- **Create ambiguity** — clues should point to the existence of a threat, not its exact nature.
- **Allow adaptive approaches** — multiple plausible theories should emerge, with misdirection possible.
- **Include risk to public knowledge** — if the player tells everyone, consequences (e.g., panic, escalation, enemy adaptation) must follow.

---

## ✅ Sample Questions the Player Might Ask

- What _exactly_ do I know so far?
- Who is the most likely to be lying?
- If I alert the town, what could go wrong?
- Who benefits from the current confusion?
- What’s the first thing I can safely rule out?
- What kind of expert would know what’s going on?
- What’s the most vulnerable point in town if something _is_ happening?

---

## 🔄 Use in Scenario Generation

When writing prompts (e.g., for GPT or DeepSeek), assume the player **will attempt to clarify, verify, and then act** based on the most logical route. If the scenario can be solved in 3 steps by talking to a single authority, players _will do that_ — unless trust or evidence prevents it.

Therefore, the design must:

- Assume the player will act as a professional investigator.
- Embed false leads, ambiguous data, and secrecy-dependent threats.
- Make players **earn clarity** through intelligent observation and questioning.
