# 🧠 When to Use **Clarity** vs **Limited Information**

| Purpose of Game Element             | Should Have Full Clarity? | Why                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Core Mechanics / Player Actions** | ✅ Yes                    | Players must understand what they _can_ do, and what actions are available.         |
| **Immediate Goal / Problem**        | ✅ Yes                    | Players need to know _what_ they’re trying to solve.                                |
| **Challenge Rules**                 | ✅ Yes                    | If players can’t learn rules, failure feels random and not skill-based.             |
| **Game State Feedback**             | ✅ Yes                    | Player needs to see the **results** of actions clearly to build mental models.      |
| **Threat Cues**                     | ⚠️ Partial                | Surface signs should be visible; true consequences or source may be hidden.         |
| **Opponent Intentions**             | ❌ No                     | Strategic tension comes from **guessing**, bluffing, or deception.                  |
| **World Systems or Metagame**       | ❌ No                     | Discovery = fun. Let players learn through experience.                              |
| **Story-Driven Mysteries**          | ❌ No                     | Player motivation often hinges on _wanting_ to understand what is currently hidden. |

---

## 🔁 Core Rule of Thumb

> **"Clarity in what you can do. Mystery in what will happen if you do it."**

### 🔹 Players must know:

- Their available actions
- Their current status
- The rules and constraints they are operating within

### 🔹 Players should **not always know**:

- What enemies or systems will do
- What others are planning
- What lies beyond the current visible layer
- What the _optimal_ strategy is

This split is what makes **thinking, experimenting, and planning meaningful.**

---

## 🎯 Clear Examples

| Design Element                                         | Clarity Level | Why?                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| `"Attack"` action does 5 damage                        | ✅ Full       | Player needs to reason with it.                 |
| Whether a character is secretly planning to betray you | ❌ Hidden     | Creates tension and surprise                    |
| Whether a door is locked                               | ✅ Full       | Player shouldn’t waste time testing brute force |
| Why your character is suddenly coughing blood          | ⚠️ Partial    | Clue-based mystery, forces deduction            |
| Whether a food crate contains poison                   | ❌ Hidden     | Encourages testing or gathering intel           |
| Whether you _can_ gather intel                         | ✅ Full       | Player must know that's a tool available        |

---

## 🧩 Strategic Use of Information Types

Here are categories of **game information** and how you might treat them:

| Info Type               | Clarity Level      | Notes                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Action Space**        | Full Clarity       | Player must see options and select them intentionally |
| **Current World State** | High Clarity       | Let players build mental models of consequences       |
| **Opponent Plans**      | Limited            | Guessing creates psychological depth                  |
| **Hidden Resources**    | Limited            | Discovery creates delight and strategic value         |
| **Backstory / Lore**    | Limited            | Optional discovery = immersion not confusion          |
| **System Rules**        | Clear through play | Learnable through observation, not needing tooltips   |

---

## ⚠️ Where Ambiguity Fails

A challenge becomes **not fun** when:

| Bad Design Pattern                  | Resulting Emotion           |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Goal is unclear                     | Confusion, apathy           |
| Failure happens with no explanation | Frustration, disempowerment |
| Outcome appears random              | Loss of trust in fairness   |
| Tools/actions are hidden            | Paralysis or brute force    |
| No feedback from action             | Lack of satisfaction        |

Your players should _always feel like they’re learning_ — not guessing blindly.

## ✅ How to Use This in Your Game

You’re already designing a world with hidden plans, alliances, betrayal, resource timing, etc. Here’s how to apply this principle:

### Clarity Layer (Must Be Clear)

- What actions your character can take
- What rules govern systems (you can eat food, you lose energy when cold)
- What resources you control
- What a threat _looks like_ (visual or behavioral cue)
- What winning and losing **looks like** (final showdown logic)

### Mystery Layer (Should Be Hidden)

- Who is plotting against you
- Which threats are fake and which are real
- What events are _about_ to trigger
- What tools other players have
- How many layers deep the systems go
- Which actions will cause unintended consequences (but give clues)

---

## 🧠 Final Mental Model: The “Fog of War” Principle

Use the metaphor of **fog of war**:

- The **action space and rules** = well-lit
- The **environment and dynamics** = partially visible, testable
- The **enemy’s thoughts and intent** = mostly obscured

> A fun game is one where players say:
> “I know what I can do… but I don’t know what will happen if I do it — and that’s exciting.”
