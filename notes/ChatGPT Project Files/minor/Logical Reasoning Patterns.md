# 🧠 Logical Reasoning Patterns

## 🔹 1. Direct Deduction (Modus Ponens)

**If A implies B, and A is true, then B must be true.**  
Used when a condition guarantees a result.

> _Example: If only the king’s steward knows the recipe, and the stew tasted of poison, then the steward must know of the poison._

---

## 🔹 2. Contrapositive (Modus Tollens)

**If A implies B, and B is false, then A must be false.**  
Eliminates possibilities by showing consequences did not occur.

> _Example: If the door was opened, the dogs would bark. The dogs did not bark → the door wasn't opened._

---

## 🔹 3. Elimination (Process of Elimination)

**If all but one option can be ruled out, the remaining must be true.**  
Relies on known impossibilities.

> _Example: Of the three who could access the vault, two were elsewhere. The third is the thief._

---

## 🔹 4. Syllogism

**If A implies B, and B implies C, then A implies C.**  
Links multiple implications into a conclusion.

> _Example: All assassins wear grey. The killer wore grey → the killer may be an assassin._

---

## 🔹 5. Transitive Relations

**If A > B and B > C, then A > C.**  
Used in comparisons of time, space, or ability.

> _Example: The sound was louder in Hall A than Hall B, and louder in B than C → Hall A is closest to the source._

---

## 🔹 6. Mutual Exclusivity

**If A and B cannot both be true, and A is true, then B must be false.**

> _Example: If only one person can wield the enchanted blade, and the knight did, then the prince did not._

---

## 🔹 7. Symmetry

**If A is related to B, then B is related to A (for symmetrical relationships).**

> _Example: If Lord Edric is married to Lady Vala, then Lady Vala is married to Lord Edric._

---

## 🔹 8. Before/After Deduction

**Reasoning based on event sequence.**

> _Example: The crow cawed before the bell rang. The bell rang at sunrise → the crow cawed before sunrise._

---

## 🔹 9. Timeline Reconstruction

**Building a consistent timeline from various accounts to test for contradictions.**

> _Example: If the scribe saw her in the garden after the murder, she cannot have done it unless she moved quickly._

---

## 🔹 10. Reductio ad Absurdum (Proof by Contradiction)

**Assume something is true → show it leads to contradiction → it must be false.**

> _Example: If the healer never left, how was she seen buying herbs in town?_

---

## 🔹 11. Counterfactual Reasoning

**What would be true if a known fact were different?**

> _Example: If the guards had been alert, the thief would have been caught. They weren't → the thief had an advantage._

---

## 🔹 12. Inductive Pattern Recognition

**Noticing repeated behaviors or coincidences that suggest a rule.**

> _Example: Each time the bell tolls thrice, a noble dies → the bell may signal assassinations._

---

## 🔹 13. Correlation Implies Suspicion

**When two things often occur together, investigate their connection.**

> _Example: Whenever the minstrel visits, someone falls ill. He may be poisoning them._

---

## 🔹 14. Negative Space Reasoning

**What should be present but isn’t?**

> _Example: No footprints by the window, though it snowed overnight → the window was likely undisturbed._

---

## 🔹 15. Constraint Satisfaction

**Fitting all known facts into a consistent arrangement.**

> _Example: The killer must be someone with a key, who wasn’t seen at the banquet, and owns a blade → only the spymaster fits._

---

## 🔹 16. Nested Deduction

**Solving smaller deductions to unlock larger truths.**

> _Example: Determine who moved the goblet → reveals who had access to the poison → reveals the killer._

---

## 🔹 17. Triangulation

**Cross-referencing multiple sources to confirm or deny a fact.**

> _Example: The stablehand heard shouting, the maid saw blood, the guard saw someone flee → together these confirm a timeline._

---

## 🔹 18. Anomaly Detection

**Spotting what breaks the pattern.**

> _Example: All the goblets are on the left, except one → why was it moved?_

---

## 🔹 19. Forced Conclusion

**When all paths of reasoning lead to the same unavoidable truth.**

> _Example: No one else had access, no one else had motive → she must be guilty._

---

## 🔹 20. Set Theory / Grouping

**Organizing people or items by traits to isolate anomalies.**

> _Example: Only those with muddy boots went outside → the lord with clean boots lied._

---

## 🔹 21. Dependency Chains

**This truth depends on another truth → follow the chain.**

> _Example: If the ritual happened, someone lit the brazier. The brazier was unlit → no ritual occurred._

---

## 🔹 22. Motive-Opportunity-Method (MOM)

**The classic triangle: Who had reason, means, and a chance?**

> _Example: She needed the money, had access to the vault, and no alibi → strong suspect._

---

## 🔹 23. Deception Deduction

**Finding contradictions between a claim and known facts.**

> _Example: He claimed to be asleep, but the servant saw him dressed and walking at dawn._

---

## 🔹 24. Recursive Reasoning

**Thinking about what others know, suspect, or plan.**

> _Example: If the mage suspects I know his secret, he may try to silence me._

---

## 🔹 25. Bayesian Updating (Plausibility Revision)

**Adjusting beliefs as new evidence is revealed.**

> _Example: The bard seemed innocent, but after the cloak was found in his room, his guilt became more likely._
